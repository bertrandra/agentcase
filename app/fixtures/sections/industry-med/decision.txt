How this verdict is computed — method & hypotheses
1 · PIPELINE

The engine never sees a single point estimate. The Monte Carlo layer perturbs the uncertain parameters, evaluates the DOM-free calculation core for each draw, and produces a distribution of the horizon-aware net benefit (Σ annual savings over the horizon − setup investment). The Decision Intelligence layer then applies business rules to the statistics of that distribution — P(net>0), P10, CVaR — plus business context (product status, glass ceiling, readiness). One verdict comes out, carrying its own risk figures.

2 · DECISION RULES (EDITABLE BENCHMARKS)
Verdict	Trigger	Meaning
✅ Recommended	P(net>0) ≥ 80% and P10 > −50% of setup	High confidence and the plausible downside stays within half the committed setup
⚠️ Conditionally viable	P(net>0) ≥ 60%	Viable only if the conditions derived from the top sensitivity drivers hold
⛔ Defer	P(net>0) < 60%	Confidence too low to commit — de-risk first (readiness, scope, ceiling)

Coherence invariant (enforced in code): a Recommended verdict is impossible when P(net<0) > 20%, whatever the rules above conclude. Because the recommendation is computed from the distribution, "Recommended" and "worst case very risky" can never coexist on screen.

3 · HYPOTHESES & ASSUMPTIONS
Sampling: each uncertain parameter is drawn from a symmetric triangular-like distribution centred on its current value (mean-preserving), clamped to its valid range. Spreads are editable benchmarks, not measured variances.
Independence: parameters are sampled independently — no correlation structure (e.g. a high ramp does not imply high token volume). Correlated risks are therefore under-represented; treat P10 as indicative, not a stress-test floor.
Metric: the outcome is the deterministic core evaluated per draw — every model assumption (dedicated-team doctrine, posture-driven automation depth, relocation pricing, agentic run-team sizing policy (hybrid floor + per-agent ops by default; legacy base-team mode available in Build → Run-team sizing) propagates into the distribution unchanged.
Reproducibility: the RNG is seeded (mulberry32); same seed + same scenario ⇒ same distribution. Changing the seed probes sampling noise (typically small vs the spread choices).
Confidence ≠ probability of success: "82% confidence" means 82% of simulated draws are net-positive under the declared uncertainty ranges — not an objective success probability. Widen the ranges and confidence mechanically drops.
Sensitivity: conditions are derived from Pearson correlations (linear, marginal). Strong non-linear or interaction effects may be under-ranked.
Freshness: the verdict is fingerprinted against the full scenario; if anything model-relevant changes, it is flagged stale and re-run (automatically or on demand) — a verdict never silently outlives its scenario.

All thresholds (80% / 60% / 20% / −50% of setup) are consulting benchmarks to validate with the client's risk appetite — like every other coefficient in AgentCase.
